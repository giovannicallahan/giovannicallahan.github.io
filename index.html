About me

From 2017 to 2022 I served in the U.S. Marine Corps as a Navigational Aids Technician. My team set up, aligned and calibrated navigation systems in demanding conditions, and as an instructor I trained other Marines to do the same. That work was about turning noisy signals into readings you can trust. Psychology asks a version of the same question about people.

Now I study psychology at Arizona State University, where I hold a 4.0 GPA and expect to finish my B.S. in spring 2027. I will be the first person in my family to earn a bachelor's degree.

As an undergraduate researcher in Dr. Iman Feghhi's lab, I lead the development of a study on what happens when people face cognitive and physical demands at the same time. I programmed the experiment in PsychoPy and help train the research assistants who run it.

I am working toward a Ph.D. in cognitive or computational neuroscience, and I am looking for research assistant and post-baccalaureate positions that begin in 2027.
